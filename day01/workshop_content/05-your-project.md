# 05 · Your group project

**Goal:** repeat the Part C workflow for a disease of your choice and defend three candidate targets. Nothing new to learn — you change one disease ID and rerun.

Work in groups. Use **Part D** of the notebook.

## What to do

1. **Find your disease ID.** In the Part D cell, change the disease name and run it; copy the ID from the first column (for example `MONDO_0011122`).
2. **Set it in Part C.** Replace the disease ID at the top of Part C with yours, in quotes.
3. **Rerun Part C.** Click that cell, then use **Runtime ▸ Run after** to run every cell below it.
4. **Choose three candidate targets.** For each, note: evidence (overall score, genetic association), drugs already acting on it (any disease), the best modality, subcellular location, expression, and an example compound series from ChEMBL if one exists.
5. **Prepare a 5-minute presentation** (3–4 slides).

## Disease ideas

- Obesity, beyond the incretin (GLP-1, GIP) receptors
- Alzheimer's disease, beyond amyloid-β, acetylcholinesterase and NMDA receptors
- Asthma, deciding for each target whether an inhaled small molecule or an injected antibody fits best
- Rheumatoid arthritis, beyond the TNF, IL-6 and JAK inhibitors
- Schizophrenia, beyond the dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors
- A disease from your own research, with the same question

## What makes a strong answer

Not the top three rows of a table. Explain the evidence behind each target, say whether the drug should block or activate it, match the modality to where the target sits, and name one risk for each — including, where it applies, that the top-ranked gene may not be the causal one.
