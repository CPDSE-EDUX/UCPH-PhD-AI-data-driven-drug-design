# 09 · Think about interactions and cavities

**Goal:** write testable predictions before drawing candidates.

For each proposed change, trace a short chain of reasoning: **observation → modification → predicted consequence → possible failure**.

1. Identify an LSD interaction that you want to preserve.
2. Identify an accessible position where a modest modification could change packing or polarity.
3. Compare the corresponding region in 5-HT2B. Use the sequence alignment to distinguish a residue substitution from a different side-chain conformation.
4. Write one affinity hypothesis and one selectivity hypothesis in the worksheet.
5. Decide what a supportive docking pose would look like before seeing scores.

| Hypothesis | Sentence to complete |
| --- | --- |
| Affinity | “Changing ___ could improve 5-HT2A binding because ___.” |
| Selectivity | “The same change could be less favorable in 5-HT2B because ___.” |
| Failure mode | “This could fail if ___, or if the ligand loses ___.” |
| Observable docking outcome | “We expect the pose to preserve ___ and place ___ near ___.” |

Consider steric fit, orientation of polar groups, loss of existing interactions, flexibility, solvent exposure and desolvation. Extra volume is not automatically useful space, and adding a hydrogen-bond donor or acceptor does not guarantee a net binding benefit.

Keep initial designs interpretable: a small number of changes makes it easier to connect an outcome to the original hypothesis. These are computational design ideas; synthesis and biological activity are outside the exercise.

**Checkpoint:** your partner can explain the predicted benefit and a plausible reason it might fail.
